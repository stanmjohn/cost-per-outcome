# Cost per outcome: Year Up, counted in earnings

One outcome is $1,000 of added earnings over seven years · Eight offices in nine US cities · Enrolled 2013 to 2014, followed for seven years

## The read

$1,000 of added earnings over seven years costs about **$606** in the middle run. The middle 80 percent of 20,000 runs fall between **$540** and **$685**.

The assumption that moves the answer most is **earnings added per person over seven years**. That is where the next research dollar goes.

## The bare number, and why it is not the answer

Budget cost divided by the gross result gives **$154** for $1,000 of added earnings over seven years. That figure credits the program with everything that happened to its participants, including what happens with no program at all. It has no range and no comparison. It is printed here once, only to be set beside the read above.

## The four questions

**What happens without the program.** The file takes the added result straight from a study with a comparison group: 38,152 likely, 31,641 to 44,663.

**Where the cost line falls.** Cost per participant is $23,100 at the likely values, in dollars as the report prints them. The report does not state a dollar year in the passages read, though it sets other programs' costs in 2014 dollars to compare them with its own. $6,750 of that, 20 percent of the full cost, sits outside the program's own budget. The file subtracts $10,800 in costs the comparison group ran up elsewhere.

**How long the result has to last.** Measured at: Years 1 to 7 after the lottery, including the training year, when earnings fell. The file gives no share still holding the result, so the tool took it as 100 percent. If results fade, the true cost is higher.

**How good the count is.** Counted by: National Directory of New Hires wage records for 1,637 program and 858 comparison group members. The file gives no count check, so the tool took the count at face value.

## How the range was made

Each assumption above was drawn 20,000 times from a triangle-shaped spread that never goes below its low or above its high and lands most often near its likely value. Each run computes cost per participant divided by the result the program added per participant. The middle run is the median. The range is the 10th to the 90th percentile. The seed is 20260917, so the same file gives the same brief on any machine.

## What this brief refuses to do

It never prints a cost per outcome without a range. It never compares this program to another one, because two programs rarely count the same outcome the same way. It never projects past the years the evidence covers. It never uses a benchmark without naming it and its source.
